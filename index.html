Let’s see what happens to a study without an analysis plan.

Planning how you will analyze your data helps reduce the type of flexibility in a study that inserts bias or chance by ensuring choices are intentional and specified in advance. Even small, exploratory, or pre-clinical studies benefit from documenting analytic decisions before the study is run.

Your Goal

In this activity, you will review a study that has no analysis plan and make decisions about how to run the study. You will get to repeat the study with a plan in place.

What’s the situation now?

  • Nothing on record says was your primary outcome.
  • There is no documentation for the statistical model you intended to fit.
  • Others reading your report cannot tell which decisions you made before you saw the data because you made all of them after.

An unplanned null is hard to publish and less likely to be included in a meta-analysis because it is easy to wave away the evidence as if nothing was learned.

A planned study that results in a null, however, still has a finding.

Others can build on it or design against it precisely because they know what you were looking for before you looked.

Were your decisions planned, exploratory, or post-hoc?

Label each decision you made.

  • Planned means specified in writing before the data existed.
  • Exploratory means a search you are willing to declare as a search.
  • Post-hoc means chosen after seeing results.
